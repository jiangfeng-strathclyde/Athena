# Evidence-led paper framing

These templates are original Athena guidance. Bracketed fields are placeholders,
not findings, citations, or claims about a real study. Use only the sections
supported by the user's materials; leave unverified items explicitly pending.

## Claim-to-evidence worksheet

| Proposed claim | Supporting artifact and location | Conditions tested | Remaining uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Specific contribution] | [Derivation, code, figure, or table] | [Data, assumptions, budget] | [Missing test or boundary] |

Separate a mathematical result from an empirical observation. For computational
claims, identify the operation being counted and the total implemented workload.
An efficient subroutine alone does not establish an end-to-end speedup.

## One-page outline

1. Problem: who needs the result, and what constraint matters?
2. Gap: what remains unresolved in the cited work?
3. Contribution: what artifact, derivation, or evaluation does this study add?
4. Evidence: which result supports each contribution?
5. Scope: under which conditions was it derived or measured?
6. Open questions: what would require further experiments?

For a short letter, combine related points instead of forcing a fixed number
of contributions. Confirm venue length and formatting requirements separately.

## Example contribution patterns

Method: "We derive [evaluation procedure] for [specific coupled operation],
reducing [measured or proven cost] under [stated assumptions]."

Evidence: "Across [documented cases], [method] achieves [verified result]
relative to [baseline] using [matched evaluation conditions]."

Boundary: "The sensitivity analysis identifies [observed failure regime] and
quantifies [verified degradation measure]."

These are sentence structures, not evidence. Omit reductions, improvements,
deployment implications, or numerical values that have not been established.

## When results are mixed

Keep the primary metric and comparison set fixed. Report wins, ties, and losses
with uncertainty where available. A useful specialization claim identifies the
regime and its supporting cases. A trade-off claim needs measurements of both
quantities; a Pareto claim needs an explicitly defined comparison set.

## Revision consistency check

Check that the abstract, introduction, results, conclusion, and response letter
describe the same contribution and assumptions. For every changed manuscript
passage, pair its location with its exact revised wording in the response when
requested. State the revision positively and concisely; record unresolved
reviewer requests separately instead of implying they have been satisfied.
